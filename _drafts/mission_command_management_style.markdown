**Thesis:** Mission command is an effective management style for high-autonomy engineering and scientific teams operating under uncertainty, yet it is largely absent from popular management style taxonomies.

- Brief anecdote: took a standard management style quiz.
- Many questions did not have appropriate answers for my management style.
- Was classified as "democratic," but this did not seem right.
- My management style is "I give you objectives and constraints, you give me results."
- Observation: most quizzes classify *personality tendencies*, not *operational doctrine*.
- Transition: some management styles are not personality-based at all.

## What Management Style Quizzes Actually Measure

- Summarize typical classifications:
  - Authoritative vs democratic
  - Coaching vs delegating
  - Task-oriented vs people-oriented
- Core critique:
  - Focus on temperament and interpersonal preference
  - Assume centralized authority
  - Rarely model high-uncertainty environments

## What Is Mission Command?

- Origin in military doctrine: Auftragstaktik to modern mission command
- Core principles:
  - Commander’s intent
  - Decentralized execution
  - Shared situational awareness
  - Disciplined initiative
  - Trust as a prerequisite

## Why Mission Command Doesn’t Appear on Quizzes

- Structural doctrine, not personality trait
- Assumes distributed expertise
- Requires comfort with ambiguity
- Separates intent from method
- Optimizes for adaptability, not control
- Contrast with quizzes that classify interpersonal style

## Why It Fits Engineering and Scientific Teams

- Environments where local expertise exceeds manager expertise
- Incomplete information, rapidly changing plans
- Innovation requires autonomy
- Mission command enables:
  - Leaders set intent, not implementation
  - Teams closest to the problem decide
  - Fast, local feedback loops
  - Initiative rewarded, not punished

## Benefits

- Scalable decision-making
- Faster responses under uncertainty
- Higher ownership and morale
- Resilience when plans fail
- Better utilization of expertise

## Risks and Failure Modes

- Intent unclear
- Trust absent
- Teams lack competence
- Leadership avoids accountability
- Organization punishes initiative

## When Not to Use It

- Highly regulated, low-variance environments
- Training-heavy junior teams
- Crisis requiring strict coordination
- Organizations without cultural maturity

## Conclusion

- Quiz didn’t capture this style
- People may misclassify themselves
- Engineering leaders should study doctrine, not personality taxonomies
