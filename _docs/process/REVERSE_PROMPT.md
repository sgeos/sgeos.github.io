### Framing

The article is the bridge between a registered, eligible company and the proposal. It divides into two tasks, finding the opportunity, which means hunting a matching topic at a directed agency and assessing fit within a broad area at an open one, and reading the solicitation, the document that states what the agency wants and the rules under which it will be judged. The organizing idea is that the topic is where the company's risk-reduction proposal meets the agency's need and the solicitation is the contract for the competition, so a misread topic wastes a good idea and a misread rule throws away a good proposal.

## Release Announcement

New Blog Post: Finding a Topic and Reading an SBIR or STTR Solicitation

The eligibility article cleared the two gates, so the company is allowed to compete and registered to submit. What it does not yet have is a target, and this article is about finding one and reading the document that governs it.

Key takeaways:
- Finding an opportunity means hunting a matching topic at a directed agency and assessing fit within a broad area at an open one, and the company reads the whole solicitation rather than only the topic.
- The solicitation changes after release, so a company tracks its amendments and the binding answers in the question period rather than the version it first downloaded.
- The best evidence for whether a topic is a fit and winnable is the searchable record of past awards, and the pre-release window is the moment to ask the topic author whether an approach fits, before the contact blackout.
- The cheapest loss is a compliance error, since a proposal that runs a page long or arrives a minute late earns the same score as a blank one, so the administrative rules deserve the same care as the technical content.
