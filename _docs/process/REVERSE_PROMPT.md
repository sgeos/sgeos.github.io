## Release Announcement

New Blog Post: Von Neumann Probes

Can humanity build a self-replicating spacecraft, and if so, when? This article traces the engineering path from von Neumann's 1948 theoretical proof through seven decades of development to a defensible timeline estimate for the first prototype.

Key takeaways:
- Von Neumann proved that self-replicating machines are theoretically possible in 1948, and biology demonstrated their physical achievability 3.5 billion years ago. The remaining challenge is engineering, not physics.
- The closure problem, the ability to manufacture 100 percent of a machine's components from raw materials, is the central engineering bottleneck. Current estimates place achievable closure at 70 to 96 percent, with semiconductor fabrication and precision optics as the hardest remaining gaps.
- The first prototype von Neumann probe is estimated to be achievable in the range of 2060 to 2130, with reductions in replication time as strategically important as increases in propulsion speed for galaxy colonization.
