## Release Announcement

New Blog Post: Phase III and the Valley of Death for SBIR and STTR

The Phase II article ended with a prototype and a commercialization plan, a result ready to become a product but not one yet. This article is about the last and hardest step, Phase III, and the chasm in front of it.

Key takeaways:
- Phase III is a destination rather than an award, since it carries no program money, so the company must cross from a funded prototype to a self-sustaining product or a fielded program on other money.
- The program leaves two tools for the crossing, the sole-source authority that lets any federal customer buy the result directly without competing, and the data rights that keep that position the company's own, but the authority is permission to buy and not a commitment.
- The crossing is made by a government transition into a funded program, often through a prime contractor, or by a market entry to paying customers, or, best of all, by both at once.
- A funded research result becomes a business at Phase III or it does not, and the companies that cross are the ones that planned for the valley from the first proposal rather than discovering it at the prototype's edge.
