## The Weight Rather Than the Shape Is the Majority of the Signature

**Integrating the lift term of the equivalent area over the whole aeroplane leaves the
weight divided by twice the dynamic pressure, and every trace of shape has gone.** At the
design condition that is **4.232 square metres, a body 2.32 metres across**, against an
aeroplane whose fuselage is visibly slimmer.

**A designer may move that area along the length and may not remove it**, because removing
it means weighing less or flying faster and lower.

**AND THE TRADE WITH ALTITUDE RUNS THE WRONG WAY.** The lift term is inversely proportional
to dynamic pressure, so climbing to lengthen the propagation path enlarges the equivalent
area the lift contributes. **It is 2.06 times larger at fifty-five thousand feet than at
forty thousand.** The two things altitude does to a sonic boom are opposed, and the article
labels the reading of that as its own inference.

## The Carpet Was Computed by Writing a Ray Tracer, and the Tracer Was Checked

**The cutoff condition in the draft was written for a ray going straight down and the
interesting rays do not go straight down.** Taking the wave normal around the Mach cone at
azimuth and using the invariance of the horizontal slowness gives a lateral cutoff azimuth
of **45.4 degrees**, and integrating the ray path down through the standard atmosphere gives
a carpet **59.2 kilometres wide**. **The boom lands 19.29 kilometres behind the aeroplane
that made it.**

**A TRACER NOBODY HAS CHECKED IS NOT A MEASUREMENT.** The literature's rule of thumb is a
statute mile of carpet per thousand feet of altitude, quoted for the Mach 2 transports the
subject grew up around. **The same tracer at Mach 2 gives 85.0 kilometres against the rule's
88.5, short by 4.0 percent**, and the check is asserted in the calculation module rather
than merely reported. **That agreement is the whole of the evidence for the Mach 1.4
answer**, and it explains it, since the Mach 1.4 carpet is 69.7 percent of the Mach 2 one.

## Length Is Cheaper Than the Draft Said

**The draft treated slenderness as a cost and half of that was wrong.** Sears-Haack wave drag
falls as the fourth power of length while wetted area rises as the first, so against a twenty
metre body of the same volume the X-59's 30.18 metres **carry a wave drag smaller by a factor
of 5.18 and a wetted area larger by a factor of 1.51**. **An exponent of four beats an
exponent of one**, and supersonic aeroplanes were long and thin before anybody cared what
they sounded like.

## Loudness Is Set by the Rise Time and Classical Theory Is Wrong by Two Orders of Magnitude

**The Taylor shock thickness for the published overpressure gives a rise time of 5.2

**What makes up the difference is molecular relaxation and atmospheric turbulence, and the

relaxation alone is insufficient. **A tenfold rise time is worth 19.9 decibels at a thousand

**So the quantity that governs the answer is not under the designer's control.** A predicted
perceived level is a prediction about the weather as much as about the aeroplane, which is
why the second phase of the programme is to fly over real communities rather than to compute
harder.
