### Shielding materials, space shielding and reactor shields in general

**The largest cluster, and the shield considered as a material.** Concrete, glass, metal hydride, polymer composite and heavy-metal shields, the general reactor shielding texts, and the shielding of crewed spacecraft. **95 titles concern shielding glasses and 75 concretes, mortars, cements or bricks**, which is the shield treated as a materials subject rather than as a design weight.

### Nuclear ramjets, rockets and space reactors

**The descendant in which the shield is affordable.** Nuclear thermal rockets and the Rover and NERVA engines, nuclear electric propulsion, the Kilopower and fission surface power reactors, and the Tory nuclear ramjet reactors of Project Pluto. **394 titles concern the nuclear thermal rocket and 154 nuclear electric propulsion, against 45 on the nuclear ramjet**, the air-breathing engine the article treats as the exception that proves the rule.

### Molten salt and circulating fuel reactors

**The Aircraft Reactor Experiment's direct line.** The Molten Salt Reactor Experiment, breeder studies, fuel salt chemistry, circulating-fuel kinetics, safeguards and the commercial designs now in development. **528 of its 843 records date from 2015 or later**, and 103 name the Molten Salt Reactor Experiment itself, the aircraft reactor's successor at Oak Ridge.

### Compact, mobile and modular reactors

**Reactors built to work away from a power station.** Microreactors, heat pipe reactors, small modular reactors, the high-temperature gas-cooled line, the Army package power reactors and nuclear ship propulsion. **164 titles name a microreactor and 131 concern ships, submarines or naval propulsion**, the two places where the article finds the X-6's constraint relaxed.

### Hydrogen, synthetic fuel and other routes to long range

**What answered the mission instead.** Hydrogen-fuelled aircraft, synthetic and sustainable aviation fuel, aerial refuelling and the solar-powered long-endurance aircraft. **173 titles concern aerial refuelling or the tanker, 144 concern hydrogen and 128 concern solar-powered or long-endurance flight.** Aerial refuelling is the arrangement the article credits with making unlimited range a matter of logistics rather than of propulsion.

### Attenuation physics, buildup and radiation transport

**The physics the attenuation law stands on.** Buildup factors, removal cross sections, attenuation coefficients, duct streaming, skyshine, and point-kernel and Monte Carlo transport. **126 titles concern the buildup factor**, which the article's lead estimate takes as a single factor of ten and which this literature tabulates by energy, depth, material and geometry.

### Decay heat, kinetics and reactor safety

**The heat that does not stop.** Fission-product decay heat measurement and standards, decay heat removal, reactor kinetics and accident analysis. **293 of its 402 records name decay heat, afterheat or decay heating**, almost all of it for reactors that stay where they are built, which is taken up under Thin Subjects.

### Shield design, optimisation and weight

**The shield as an engineering problem.** Shield design methods, minimum-weight and shadow shield optimisation, reactor-specific shield designs, and the Oak Ridge Tower Shielding, Lid Tank and Bulk Shielding facilities. **The cluster's median publication year is 1972**, and 56 titles concern those three facilities, two of which appear in the programme's own reports.

### Reactor fuel, cooling and the wider nuclear literature

**The residual, reported and not hidden.** Reactor work that belongs to no heading above, including fuel elements and their fabrication, fission product release, liquid-metal-cooled reactors, the Plum Brook test reactor, gaseous-core and pulsed propulsion concepts, and general surveys of nuclear propulsion that span ships, aircraft and rockets at once.

### The nuclear aircraft and its programme record

**The programme's own record, and the reason this survey reaches into the Office of Scientific and Technical Information.** Quarterly and semiannual progress reports, the Aircraft Reactor Experiment, the Heat Transfer Reactor Experiments, the Aircraft Shield Test Reactor, the nuclear turbojet studies and the later proposals for nuclear-powered transports. **205 of its 265 records date from 1961 or earlier**, the year the programme was cancelled, so most of what was written about the aircraft was written while it was being attempted.

### Radiation effects on materials, components and electronics

**What the reactor does to the aeroplane around it.** Radiation damage to lubricants, elastomers, plastics, metals and electronic components. **13 titles name the aircraft**, among them studies of aircraft lubricants and fuels, tyres, transparent materials and tank sealants.

### Crew dose and the human limit

**The dose the crew was allowed, and a literature that measures a different one.** Crew radiation exposure, radiation effects in people and primates, and the shielding of space crews. **50 of its 66 records name aircrew or cosmic radiation, and every one of them concerns the natural radiation field at altitude**, so the modern literature on dose in flight is about the sky rather than about a source carried aboard.

### Similarity, information and the design of experiments

**The survey rests on 5,703 research records drawn from Crossref, the NASA Technical Reports Server, the Defense Technical Information Center and the Office of Scientific and Technical Information**, of which 3,186, being 55.9 percent, are report primaries by the identifier rule set out under The Definition of Primary. **643 of the 5,603 dated records, being 11.5 percent, were published between 1946 and 1961**, the life of the programme, and 2,104, being 37.6 percent, date from 2015 or later. The median publication year is 1999, and the records fall into 13 clusters.

### The Definition of Primary

**Primacy is read from the identifier.** A record is a report primary when its address is a NASA Technical Reports Server citation, a record of the Office of Scientific and Technical Information, or a digital object identifier under the 10.2172 prefix, which Crossref registers for the Department of Energy reports that office holds, or under the 10.21236 prefix, which it registers for Defense Technical Information Center reports. **One Department of Energy report is reachable both as an office record and as a 10.2172 identifier**, and the survey cites each such report once. Of the 3,186 report primaries, 2,658 are Department of Energy reports, 438 are NASA reports and 90 are Defense Technical Information Center reports.

**The rule does not reach the journal papers that the programme's own engineers published**, so 55.9 percent is a lower bound on how much of the survey is first-hand. The documents that carry the argument are the progress reports and comprehensive technical reports named above, and every one of them is a report primary by this rule.
