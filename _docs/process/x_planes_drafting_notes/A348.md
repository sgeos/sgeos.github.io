### Three Sweep-Store Patterns Had to Be Switched Off, and the Warning to Do So Was Written One Article Ago

**This corpus keeps a shared store of homonym patterns, and a pattern earned in one article is not automatically valid in the next.** The X-49 article recorded `ramjet` as a contaminant, because a survey of helicopter ducted propulsors kept returning solid ducted rockets. The X-50 article recorded `hypersonic`, `scramjet` and `missile` as contaminants of a rotorcraft survey.

**Both entries carried a written warning that they must not be reused in the articles where they are the subject**, and both named this series' hypersonic articles specifically.

**Those three patterns would have deleted nearly half of everything harvested.** Run again on 8 October 2026 over the 7,151 records of the first harvest, with the store as it now stands, they refuse 3,462, being 48.4 percent. The sample includes scramjet combustor flameholding, ramjet to scramjet mode transition, waverider aerodynamics and inlet unstart prediction, which is to say the article.

**A warning addressed to a reader is not a mechanism.** The three patterns now carry tags, a caller switches them off by name, and an unknown tag raises rather than being ignored, so a typo cannot silently leave a filter armed against the article's own subject. **Every other pattern in the store stayed armed**, because turbomachinery, wind energy and atmospheric chemistry are contaminants here too.

### Two Subjects Were Aimed At Directly and Did Not Move

### A Diagnostic of Mine Reported Two Records Where There Were Many More

**A probe for the Hyper-X programme was written as `hyper-?x` and the corpus normalises an intraword hyphen to a space before matching**, so the pattern could not match `Hyper X` and reported the sibling programme as almost absent from a pool that held many more records of it. ### The Instrument Built for the Previous Article Caught Six Errors in This One

### A Presence Check Passed for the Wrong Reason

**The conclusion described the fuel's heat-sink capacity as about a twentieth of the energy it releases and it is not.** The range is 5.4 to 8.1 percent, which is one part in 18.5 to one part in 12.3, so the correct statement is between a twelfth and an eighteenth.

**Correcting it left a claim written in words, and adding the digits 12 and 18 to the number checker made that check pass on `18.8`, on `18,500` and on the X-12 backlink in the opening sentence.** It went green without checking anything.

**That is the defect class this corpus has been chasing since the X-45 article**, where a presence check confirmed a stale figure because the stale figure was still present. The checker now verifies spelled-out ordinals as words, and it confirms two further claims that were previously unverifiable, being that the stagnation temperature ratio of 3.15 is a third and the heating rate ratio of 6.67 is a seventh.
