### The Definition of Primary

**Primacy is a property of the document, and this survey derives it from the identifier.** A record is a report primary when it is a NASA Technical Reports Server record or carries a digital object identifier under the 10.21236 prefix, which Crossref registers for Defense Technical Information Center reports. By that rule 1,643 primaries are Technical Reports Server records and 386 are Defense Technical Information Center reports.

**The rule undercounts what is first-hand.** Conference papers of the American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics, abbreviated AIAA, and journal papers by the engineers and pilots who flew the aircraft are primary by any reading and are not reached by an identifier rule, and the books and reference works that carry much of this article's history are cited in the Books and Reference lists and are not counted in the survey at all. **27.5 percent is therefore a lower bound on how much of this survey is first-hand.**

### Ground facilities against flight, and the similarity gap
**The residue this article opens with, and the largest cluster.** Comparisons of wind-tunnel, shock-tunnel and computational results with flight, Reynolds-number and scale effects, wall interference, similitude and dimensional analysis, subscale and free-flight testing, and the facilities themselves. **447 of its 910 titles, about half, name flight**, and the rest describe the facilities, the scaling laws and the corrections without a flight to compare against.
### The X-series and other research aircraft
**The series' own aircraft and their neighbours.** Titles that name an X-designation, a research airplane or experimental aircraft, a lifting body, or one of the research aircraft that flew beside the series, such as the D-558 and the XV-15. **Its titles name 34 distinct X-designations, the X-43, X-29 and X-15 most often**, and 411 of its 685 records are report primaries, the highest share of any cluster in the table, which is the NACA and NASA report series documenting its own aircraft.
### Aeroelasticity, flutter and the flexible wing
**The constraint that made the X-29 possible.** Divergence of forward-swept wings, flutter and its clearance in flight, aeroelastic tailoring, active flutter suppression and the active aeroelastic wing. **120 of its titles name a forward-swept wing and 138 name flutter suppression**, the passive answer and the active one that the review above describes as joining it.
### Compressibility, wave drag and the high-speed wing
**The transonic drag rise and what answered it.** Compressibility, the drag rise, wave drag and the area rule, supercritical aerofoils, swept and slender wings, minimum-drag bodies, and drag measured in flight. **66 titles name wave drag or the area rule and 57 a supercritical aerofoil or wing**, the two design answers to the rise derived in the drag section.
### Configurations of the recent demonstrators
**The X-57, the X-66 and their contemporaries.** Distributed electric propulsion, truss- and strut-braced wings, blended and hybrid wing bodies, morphing and variable-camber wings, and vertical take-off configurations from the tilt wing to the electric vertical take-off aircraft. **Its median year is 2016**, and vertical take-off configurations supply 139 of its titles, distributed propulsion 91, morphing and variable camber 94, braced wings 68 and blended or hybrid wing bodies 47, so the vertical-flight questions the X-13 through X-22 block opened are the largest of the five strands.

**The thermal keystone.** Aerodynamic and stagnation-point heating, atmospheric and planetary entry, ablators and thermal protection systems, and heating measured in flight, from the blunt-body relations of the 1950s to the entry instrumentation of the Mars landers.

**The question the X-series opened and did not close.** Hypersonic boundary-layer transition and its prediction, laminar flow control and natural laminar flow in flight, receptivity and transition flight experiments. **126 of its titles name hypersonic flow and 83 laminar flow**, so the hypersonic problem the review calls the largest source of uncertainty in hypersonic sizing is also the larger part of the literature.
