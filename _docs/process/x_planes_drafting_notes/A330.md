## The Source Base

**The research survey holds 6,020 research records drawn from Crossref, the NASA Technical Reports Server and the Defense Technical Information Center**, of which 1,066 are report primaries, being 17.7 percent, by the identifier rule set out under The Definition of Primary. **The median publication year is 2012.** Of the 5,979 dated records, 2,372 were published through 2001, when the programme was cancelled, being 39.7 percent, 2,882 were published in 2015 or later, being 48.2 percent, and 1,495 in 2022 or later, being 25.0 percent.

### The Definition of Primary

**Primacy is a property of the document, and this survey derives it from the identifier.** A record is a report primary when its address is on the NASA Technical Reports Server or it carries a digital object identifier under the 10.21236 prefix, which Crossref registers for Defense Technical Information Center reports. **The rule does not reach the conference papers in which much of the programme's own engineering was published**, such as the American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics papers on the X-33, so 17.7 percent is a lower bound on how much of the survey is first-hand.

**A primary source is one contemporary with the work, and for this article the work has an unusually clean boundary.**
The X-33 programme ran from the Phase II award on 2 July 1996 to cancellation in early 2001, and the reports
