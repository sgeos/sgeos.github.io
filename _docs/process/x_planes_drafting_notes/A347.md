## Reading rule and blind sample

The rule was written before any reading, in `READING_RULE.md`: a title is kept when its subject is one of fifteen subjects the argument uses (S1 to S15) for a flight vehicle, or a domain-free treatment of one; it is dropped as H (homonym), D (same physics applied to a non-flight-vehicle domain named in the title), U (a flight-vehicle subject the article does not use: icing, crashworthiness, avionics and navigation, displays and workload, noise other than tip-jet noise, maintenance and diagnostics, manufacturing and structural design, armament and stores, operations and procurement, supersonic and hypersonic flight, space) or E (editorial). One refinement, R1, was made during chunk 01 before any chunk verdict was recorded: chapters are research works, not parts of works. It was re-applied to every title already read, which changed one blind-sample verdict (item 39, Chapter 4 Thrust Augmentation, to kept).

The blind sample of 300 was drawn from all 7,409 records before any reading (`draw_sample.py`, seed 347300) and read without cluster names (`sample300_read.txt`): 57 off topic as read, 56 under R1, 18.7 percent, 95 percent Wilson interval 14.7 to 23.5 percent. The full reading found 1,424 of 7,409 off topic, 19.2 percent, inside the interval. Seven sample titles were read differently by the two readings (`sample_vs_full.txt`); all seven were sample keeps that the rule's U classes cover (a re-entry lifting body, store trajectories, a mortar, simulator software, a supersonic nozzle, flight control diagnostics, a projectile). Each was re-read under the rule and the full-reading verdict stands.
