### What the X-21 Actually Drew

The suction coefficient of $2 \times 10^{-4}$ used above is an assumption, and the record of the programme allows it to be tested. **The local suction coefficients the X-21 design called for were higher.** [Joslin 1998][ref_joslin_1998], summarising the Northrop final report, gives typical values of $v_{w}/V_{\infty}$ from $5 \times 10^{-4}$ in regions of nearly flat pressure to $10 \times 10^{-4}$ near the leading edge, and adds that at Mach 0.74 and 41,400 feet the measured suction distribution matched the predicted one in shape but exceeded it in level by 50 percent. Since the break-even coefficient above is fixed by the friction saving and the machinery, the margin at any other coefficient is simply

### What the Survey Admits

**A record is admitted only when a person reading its title finds it on this article's subject**, the subject being suction laminar flow control, the stability theory behind it, the surfaces it needs and the insects and ice that contaminate them. **The phrase boundary layer belongs to meteorology as much as to aerodynamics**, and a whole journal is devoted to the atmospheric boundary layer. Operating-room laminar air flow, crossflow filtration and a Cretaceous stick-insect fossil share this subject's vocabulary in the same way and none of them is part of it.

**One homonym in this subject lives inside aerodynamics rather than outside it.** Boundary layer control means suction to hold laminar flow and reduce drag, and it also means suction or blowing to delay separation and increase lift. They share a name, a mechanism and often an author, and they are different technologies answering different questions, so a title that names boundary layer control does not by itself place a record in this subject. Records that control separation or stall by plasma, jets, fluidic oscillators, tubercles or flexible surfaces, without suction through the wall, are excluded on that ground.

Many words this subject depends on also name something else, and a record that shares only such a word is excluded. Perforated also names panels made to absorb sound, laminar also names flames and the internal flow and heat transfer of tubes, channels and fins, and coating also names protection against corrosion and the surfacing of pavements, insulators and face masks. Non-Newtonian, magnetohydrodynamic and porous-medium flows, chemical mixers, fuel cells, surgery, seaweed and power grids share the vocabulary of the boundary layer without its subject. Transition also names the conversion of a tilting aircraft from hover to forward flight, noise also names the sound an aerofoil radiates, and attachment also names separated flows reattaching behind a step or a ramp. Bluff bodies and their wakes, turbomachinery blades and cascades, the chemistry of re-entry boundary layers and the interaction of shock waves with separated flow are excluded for the same reason. Notices of erratum, correction, retraction or withdrawal, figure, table and supplementary-file records, peer-review reports and journal front matter are excluded as well, because a survey counts research works and those are parts of works or editorial events rather than works, and a second deposit of one document is counted once.
