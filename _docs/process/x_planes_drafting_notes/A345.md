## The Source Base

**The survey rests on 7,981 research records assembled from Crossref, the NASA Technical Reports Server and the Defense Technical Information Center**, of which 1,555 are primaries, being 19.5 percent, and 1,552 of those are report primaries by the identifier rule set out under The Definition of Primary. **Thirteen primaries are cited by identifier rather than left to the sweep to find**, three of them journal and conference papers that the identifier rule does not reach, and the primary pass added nine of those thirteen.

### The Definition of Primary Was Wrong and It Was Wrong in a Measurable Way

**Primacy was being inherited from whichever sweep returned the record, and it is a property of the document.** A Defense Technical Information Center report carries a Crossref-registered digital object identifier under the 10.21236 prefix, so the same report is returned by the DTIC sweep and by the Crossref sweep, under two different labels.

**101 urls in the pool were held twice under two labels, and deduplication kept the Crossref label for 95 of them.** Each of those is a report primary that was counted as secondary. **A further 38 arrived only through Crossref and so never carried the primary label at all**, giving 133 in total.

**Primacy is now derived from the identifier**, being a NASA Technical Reports Server url or the DTIC prefix, which is the corpus rule about recomputing rather than inheriting applied to provenance instead of to arithmetic.

### The Equation Pass Made Seven Subjects Load-Bearing and Three of Them Were Thin

**The four-pass rhythm has no step that re-asks whether an absent subject has become load-bearing**, and the audit at the head of this pass is the only thing that can. The X-47 article recorded an atmosphere cluster of two as correct and its equation pass then made the atmosphere necessary. **The X-48 article's equation pass did the same thing seven times.**

### Viscosity Stayed at Zero and That Is the Fourth Kind of Thin

**Six targeted queries across three sources returned 421 records mentioning viscosity or transport properties, and the aeronautical gate admitted 14.** The remainder are lubricating oils, canola oil, molten metals, protein solutions, supercooled sucrose and the Saybolt conversion tables of the American Society for Testing and Materials.

**The gate is refusing them correctly and no loosening of it would help.** The viscosity of air as a function of temperature is not an under-served subject. **It was settled by Sutherland in 1893 and has lived in tables ever since** \[[Sutherland, The viscosity of gases and molecular force][research_sutherland_viscosity]\] \[[U.S. Standard Atmosphere, 1976][research_us_standard_atmosphere]\] \[[The NBS-NACA tables of thermal properties of gases][research_nbs_naca_tables]\].

**This is the fourth kind of thin, which is a subject whose vocabulary does not discriminate**, compounded by the third, which is a subject so settled it stopped generating papers. **A bibliographic sweep is the wrong instrument for a tabulated physical constant**, and the remedy is to know the document exists and fetch it by identifier.

### The Harvest Never Asked for the Aeroplane by Name

**The first harvest asked for the subject matter and never named the X-48.** That is why the four documents the whole argument rests on had to be cited by identifier, and it looked like foresight when it was covering for a gap.

**A supplementary harvest naming the aeroplane returned eight further programme documents**, including the two that changed this article. **One is the ground-to-flight correlation update, which answers the article's keystone question directly** and which the draft argued without \[[Vicroy, X-48B blended wing body ground to flight correlation update][research_vicroy_correlation]\]. **The other reports the phase-one flight results with the stall angles attached** \[[Taylor, X-48B preliminary flight test results][research_taylor_preliminary]\] \[[Goldthorpe, Rossitto and Hyde, X-48B blended wing body flight test performance of maximum sideslip and high to post stall angle-of-attack command tracking][research_goldthorpe_poststall]\].

**A near-miss is worth recording because it is this article's own subject.** A first measurement reported zero records naming the X-48 in a pool of six thousand, and that was going to be stated as a fact about indexing. **The pattern was `\bX-?48\b`, and a word boundary after `48` cannot match `X-48B`, because the next character is a letter.** The records had been there all along. **An article about a hyphen defeating a gate nearly shipped a claim produced by a word boundary defeating a probe.**
