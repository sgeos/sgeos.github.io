## Follow-up: payload worth of a percent of impulse (9 October)

The sentence "That is a large number for a launch vehicle, where a percent of impulse is several percent of
payload." had no basis. The rocket equation with the article's own figures does not reproduce "several".
- The second-stage payload sensitivity at fixed propellant and structure is
  $\Delta m_p/m_p = (w/c_{e,2}) \cdot m_0/(m_0-m_f) \cdot m_f/m_p > w/c_{e,2}$.
- The 2.6 km/s staging speed is taken as a floor on $\Delta v_1$.
- $c_{e,2}$ is assumed at 3,000 to 3,400 m/s, because the E2's specific impulse is not published.
- This gives at least 0.76 to 0.87 percent of payload per percent of first-stage exhaust velocity, plus two
  unpublished mass ratios.

The sentence now reads "at least three quarters of a percent". The assumptions are named in the passage
and added to the Epistemic State assumptions paragraph. Two displays were added, 53 to 55, together with
eight Symbols rows, and "Fifty-three relations" became "Fifty-five". `symcheck.py` gained the eight
symbols. `verify_numbers.py` gained `check_payload_sensitivity`, which checks the derivative by finite
difference on an arbitrary stage, the worked bound, the specific-impulse conversion, the presence of the
new wording and the absence of "several percent of payload". It passes 741 of 741. Prose words are
15,222. The added passage is

````
That is a large number for a launch vehicle, and the rocket equation says how large.

Let $\Delta v_1$ and $\Delta v_2$ be the ideal velocity increments of the two stages in metre per second, $c_{e,2}$ the effective exhaust velocity of the second stage in metre per second, $m_0$ and $m_f$ the mass of the second stage with its payload at ignition and at burnout in kilogram, $m_p$ the payload mass in kilogram, $w$ a gain in $\Delta v_1$ in metre per second, and $\Delta m_p$ the payload that gain buys in kilogram. The rocket equation is linear in exhaust velocity, so a uniform one percent rise in first-stage effective exhaust velocity raises $\Delta v_1$ by one percent. Holding the second stage's propellant and structure fixed and letting it deliver $w$ less, the rocket equation gives
