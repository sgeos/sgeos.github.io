> ## The Source Base
> **The research survey behind this article holds 4,409 research records drawn from Crossref, the NASA Technical Reports Server and the Defense Technical Information Center**, of which 388 are report primaries, being 8.8 percent, by the identifier rule set out under The Definition of Primary. **The median publication year is 2013.** Of the 4,250 dated records, 1,901, being 45 percent, were published in 2015 or later, 3,116, being 73 percent, were published in 2000 or later, and 316 predate 1980. **A survey of an instability first understood in the 1930s that is nearly three quarters contemporary, counting 2000 or later as contemporary, is telling you that the control problem is the live one**, because the aerodynamics has not changed and the actuators and the computers have.
> ### The Definition of Primary
> **Primacy is a property of the document, and this survey derives it from the identifier.** A record is a report primary when its address is on the NASA Technical Reports Server or it carries a digital object identifier under the 10.21236 prefix, which Crossref registers for Defense Technical Information Center reports. **It is a measure of where a document is hosted and not of what it is**, so 8.8 percent is a lower bound on how much of the survey is first-hand. > ### What the Survey Admits
> **A record is admitted only when a person reading its title finds it on this article's subject**, meaning the physics of flutter and its suppression, the methods by which a flutter boundary is found and measured, the flexible and tailless aeroplanes that exhibit it, the aeroelastic and flight dynamic models of such aircraft, the flight test practice that clears them, and the named vehicles of this programme and of its ancestor.
