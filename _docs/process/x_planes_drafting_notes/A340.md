### Corrections the primary record required

- National Aero-Space Plane, "cancellation in 1993", cited to a 1987 and a 1991 paper that predate it. Peebles 2007 gives approval in 1986 and cancellation in October 1994 after some 2.4 billion dollars. Corrected and cited. The X-30 article (A327) also says 1993 and was not edited.
- "The mishap investigation board convened five days after the first flight": the board report gives 5 June 2001, three days after. Corrected.
- Third flight Mach number 9.6: 9.6 is the separation target. The flight reports give a maximum powered Mach number of 9.68 and a separation at 9.736. Every flight-3 figure was recomputed at 9.68 (stagnation temperature 4,517 to 4,588 K, sonic static 3,764 to 3,824 K, total pressure ratio 32,345 to 34,178, speed 2,934 to 2,959 m/s, dynamic pressure 957 to 973 lbf/ft², specific thrust 147 to 148, Reynolds number 7.58 to 7.65 million). `tmp/a340/verify.py` now uses 9.68.
- "The two flights were flown at the same dynamic pressure to within two pounds per square foot ... the strongest available check": the reported powered dynamic pressures are about 980 and 930 lbf/ft² (Voland 2005), 50 apart, and the trajectory was designed to a 1,000 lbf/ft² target, so the agreement was arranged. Withdrawn in the body and the Epistemic State and replaced by the comparison with the reported values.
- Heating at "a three millimetre radius", a radius no source states. The documented design radius is 0.030 inch and the Mach 10 nose 0.050 inch (Ohlhorst 2005). Recomputed at those radii, 8.06 and 16.86 MW/m², ratio 2.09, and 1.62 as flown.
- "The trajectory ... was lower and faster than a typical Pegasus trajectory": the board says lower, and Peebles describes the launch as lower and slower, 20,000 ft and Mach 0.5. Corrected.
- "The board recommended exactly that" and "The board recommended in 2002": the board's recommendations are in export-controlled sections not in the public volume. Replaced by what the return-to-flight review records was done.
- "The X-43C ... was planned in detail and cancelled", cited to 2002 and 2003 papers that predate any cancellation. Now states what Grindle 2011 records, that it remained a planned demonstrator and that the X-43A project ended in December 2004.
- "The X-51A ... flew a hydrocarbon scramjet for several minutes", cited to a 2008 paper that predates the flights. Now cited to the Boeing release on the fourth flight.
- "The margin that was positive at Mach 7 was approximately zero at Mach 10 ... somewhere between Mach 7 and Mach 9.6 they crossed" and "it is usually reported away": the third flight was designed as a cruise test (Freeman 1997, Peebles 2007, Marshall 2005), and the planned Mach 10 acceleration flight was never flown. Restated as a cruise test that met its requirement, and the margin above cruise is listed as unsettled.
- "every hypersonic air-breathing vehicle designed since has been designed with methods this flight data anchors": unsupported. Replaced by the data release Voland 2005 records.
- "The agreement is qualitative in this article": the programme's comparisons are quantitative, thrust within less than two percent (Voland 2005) and three percent (Grindle and Bahm 2006), drag higher than predicted. Rewritten.
- Source Base "32 of them come from the NASA Technical Reports Server": recounted over the prose-cited sources, 31 before this pass. Now 39 of 46.
- The Inference item that the programme "is known to have changed the leading edges" is now a sourced Historical Fact.
