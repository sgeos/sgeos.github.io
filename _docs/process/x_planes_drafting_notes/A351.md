Every rate and share in this section is counted on that year, so the article's own source is enough to recount it.

**Publication ran at 25.3 records a year through the 1970s, fell to 13.8 through the 1980s, and has grown every decade since to 80.1 in the 2020s**, a decade whose count is divided by seven because the survey reaches seven years into it. The trough is 54.5 percent of the peak that preceded it, and the present rate is 5.8 times the trough. **The American supersonic transport was cancelled in 1971 and the prohibition took effect in 1973**, and the decade that follows those two dates is the only one after the 1930s in this corpus in which the yearly rate fell. Counted in records rather than records a year, the unfinished 2020s already hold 561 against 542 for the 2010s.

**The corpus is split almost evenly between the recent and the old.** 37.2 percent of the dated records were published in 2015 or later and 37.9 percent before 2000, which is a field with a long memory and an active present rather than either alone.

**The report primaries are older than the survey and their dates follow the agency's research programmes rather than the journals.** Of the 208 report primaries that carry a year, the median is 1991 against 2008 for the corpus, a gap of 17 years, and 79 date from before 1980. **The 1990s hold more of them than any other decade**, at 53. Of the 1,406 dated records published in 2000 or later, 55 are report primaries, so the recent literature is mostly journal and conference work. A report-primary fraction of 12.0 percent in a corpus whose median is 2008 is therefore reporting something about when the subject was funded.

### The Definition of Primary

**Primacy is a property of the document, and this survey derives it from the identifier.** A record is a report primary when its address is on the NASA Technical Reports Server or it carries a digital object identifier under the 10.21236 prefix, which Crossref registers for Defense Technical Information Center reports. **By that rule 289 of the 2,402 research records are report primaries, being 12.0 percent**, among them the Quiet Spike flight test report, the paper on the origins of the shaped-boom demonstration, the cutoff measurements of 1971 and the cutoff investigation of 2012.

**The programmes' own bibliographies lead to the older documents, and those documents are cited directly.** The Quiet Spike flight test report, the shaped-boom demonstration paper and the cutoff investigation each name the documents they were built on. The curated references include the Gulfstream design chain from 2003 to 2009, the classical minimisation and propagation literature from 1956 to 1979, the human-response experiments the loudness procedure was calibrated against, and the standard atmosphere the article computes in.

**The measure sees most of this article's primary documents and misses the five that decide it.** A designation registry, an executive order, a final rule in the Federal Register, a part of the code of federal regulations and a patent carry no identifier the measure can read, and those five are what the argument turns on. **The rule does not reach the journal papers among the primary sources either**, such as Whitham on weak shocks and George and Seebass on minimisation, so 12.0 percent is a lower bound on how much of the survey is first-hand. **The count of named primary documents is reported beside the fraction rather than instead of it**, and the measure is not adjusted to flatter the number.
