#### Cryptotelemetritocratic Theory

A87 was about telemeritocracy: a synthesis of telocracy and meritocracy.
A89 is about cryptotelemeritocracy: a synthesis of cryptocracy and telemeritocracy.
Cryptography has nothing to do with this synthesis.

- Cryptocratic layer: an anonymous arbitrator elected from an anonymous pool of candidates
  audits and arbitrates issues related to the telos to prevent mission drift.
  The candidate pool has the power to recall ineffective arbitrators and act as a check
  and balance in the cryptocratic layer.
  This layer ensures mission alignment.
- Telocratic layer: actors and actions are subservient to the mission.
- Meritocratic layer: resources and responsibilities are delegated to effective actors.

In a weak cryptotelemeritocracy, the candidate pool is known or can be determined.
In a strong cryptotelemeritocracy, the candidate pool is entirely anonymous to outsiders.
In a double-blind cryptotelemeritocracy,
candidates in the candidate pool are entirely anonymous to one another.
In all cases, existance of the candidate pool and arbitrator are know,
and the arbitrator's identity is anonymous to outsiders.

A weak cryptoarbitrator's identity is know to the candidate pool when elected.
A strong cryptoarbitrator's identity is unknown to the candidate pool when elected.
A double-blind cryptoarbitrator's identity is unknowable to the candidate pool
because their identity is never revealed in the first place.

The strength of the cryptotelemeritocracy and cryptoarbitrator
can theoretically be mixed and matched with varying levels of practicality.

The cryptoarbitrator has powers written into the organization,
and these powers may be sweeping depending on how the organization is chartered.
In theory, a cryptoarbitrator may be able to:

- Remove ineffective or misaligned senior management, that are bad fits for the telos.
- Veto or cancel projects that are not aligned with the telos,
  or that drawn unjustifiable resources from pursuit of the telos.
- Force acceptance of external opportunities that align with the telos.

In theory, a known party could perform these functions in a telemeritocracy.
In that case, you would simply have an arbitrator and not a crypto arbitrator.

In certain setups, the candidate pool may report to the active arbitrator.
In this case, the cryptocratic layer is embedded in the organization,
and the arbitrator is the executive.
It is important to note that the arbitrator is only justified in acting
when the organization is misaligned with the telos.
The organization must be chartered correctly to give the arbitrator
and cryptocratic layer appropriate power to enfore telos alignment.

As written, the article states that the arbitrator is drawn from the employee pool.
This is not the case.
Drawing from the employee pool is one strategy of many.
In principle, the cryptocratic layer has private procedures and
drawns from an unknown private pool of candidates.
These people could be drawn from a governing body, a family, a professional society,
or from some other pool.
The important thing is that this pool is suitable for telos alignment,
and unknown to the greater organization.

If the elected arbitrator is not working at the cryptotelemeritocratic organization,
there must be a placement mechanism.
This person would be placed as a legitimate subject matter expert in a suitable position
in a suitable department.
In principle, this person's overt placement could be anywhere from
an entry level employee to senior leadership.
This person overtly functions in their nominal official capacity,
and covertly as the active arbitrator.

If other memebers of the candidate pool have support duties,
they also participate overtly in a nominal official capacity,
and in a covert capacity that supports the arbitrator.
"Nominal" does not mean they cannot acutally perform their overt function.
Members of the cryptocratic layer are expected to be legitimate subject matter experts
or otherwise competently contributing as regular employees.

Depending on the mechanics of the cryptocratic layer,
it is conceivable that some members of the cryptocratic layer
would not be employed by the organization for one reason or another.
It is also possible to have an external active arbitrator,
but this deviates from the intent of the proposal.

Cryptomeritocry is suitable for organizations with long time horizon missions
that may take generations to achieve.
The candidates have a procedure of their own to elect the person they feel is best
qualified to enforce the telos.
They also have the power of recall if they feel they made a mistake.
In theory, it could be adopted by organizations that need controlled mission drift.
In this case, the arbitrator would likely have the power to veto mission drift.

Action compelled by the arbitrator is officially signed by the position,
but the identity of the individual is anonymous.
